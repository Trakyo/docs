---
title: First vs last touch
description: How Trakyo attributes revenue and conversions to content — and why the two modes disagree.
---

Attribution answers one question: **when money arrives, which piece of content gets the credit?**

A lead usually interacts with more than one piece of content before buying. They discover you through a YouTube video in March, click through a few emails, then book a call from an Instagram reel in May and buy. Which content "made" that sale — the video or the reel? There's no single right answer, so Trakyo gives you both perspectives:

- **First touch** — credit goes to the content that **first brought the lead in** (the March video). This view rewards *discovery*: what fills the top of your funnel.
- **Last touch** — credit goes to the content the lead **last interacted with before converting** (the May reel). This view rewards *closing*: what pushes people over the line.
- **Multi-touch (split credit)** — in the dashboard only: every piece of content in the journey gets an equal share (the video and the reel would each get half). This view rewards *everything that helped*.

## How it works in the API

Analytics endpoints accept an `attribution` query parameter:

```bash
# first_touch is the default
curl "https://api.trakyo.io/v1/metrics?period=30d&attribution=last_touch" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer tky_live_..."
```

Any other value returns [`400 invalid_attribution_mode`](/docs/errors/invalid_attribution_mode).

:::caution{title="Multi-touch is app-exclusive for now"}

The Trakyo dashboard also offers **Multi-touch (split credit)**, which splits credit equally across every touch in a lead's journey (stored as `linear`). Multi-touch is **not available via the API yet** — it's app-exclusive for now.

Also note the API **never reads your account's dashboard attribution preference** — the mode is set per request via the `attribution` parameter. If your dashboard is set to multi-touch (or last touch) and you call the API without the parameter, you get **first-touch** numbers, which will not match what you see in-app.

:::

The **content and source leaderboards return both revenues on every row** — no need for two calls:

```json
{
  "name": "How we 10x'd our funnel",
  "type": "youtube",
  "revenue": { "first_touch": "21973.00", "last_touch": "6473.00" },
  ...
}
```

## Which link earned it

`GET /v1/destinations` goes one level below content: it lists every URL your tracking links point to, with its clicks and the `form_submissions`, `bookings` and `revenue` that came through that link. Use it to compare the links under one video (apply, free training, newsletter) by what they earn, not only by clicks.

A conversion counts for a link when the content it is attributed to was reached by clicking that link:

- with `attribution=first_touch`, the lead's **earliest** click on a link of that content;
- with `attribution=last_touch`, their **latest** such click before the conversion.

Three things follow, and they are by design:

- **The links can add up to less than the content.** A lead who reached the content another way (a website visit, an ad form, an import, a manual attribution) is counted in `GET /v1/content` and in no destination row.
- **A row can have `clicks: 0`.** The link was clicked before your date range and earned a conversion inside it.
- **Revenue is net of refunds**, and a refund lands on the link its sale was credited to.

```bash
curl "https://api.trakyo.io/v1/destinations?period=30d&attribution=last_touch&currency=EUR" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer tky_live_..."
```

## Why the numbers disagree — and what that tells you

That example above is real: one video drove **$21,973 first-touch but only $6,473 last-touch**. Same video, same transactions — different question being asked.

- **High first-touch, low last-touch** → a *discovery asset*. It brings in people who buy later through other touchpoints. Cutting it would starve the funnel even though it "closes" little.
- **Low first-touch, high last-touch** → a *closer*. Retargeting ads and "final CTA" content look like this. It converts demand that other content created.
- **Total revenue is the same in both modes** — attribution never changes *how much* you earned, only *how it's distributed* across content.

## Manual overrides

`POST /v1/transactions` attributes automatically from the lead's journey. To pin a transaction to specific content, `PATCH /v1/transactions/{id}` with a single `content_item_id` (`ci_…`) — it is written to **both** first- and last-touch, and the transaction is marked `attribution_is_manual: true`. (The `first_touch_content_item_id` / `last_touch_content_item_id` fields on transaction responses are read-only; attribution can be changed but not cleared via the API.)
