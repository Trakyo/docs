A transaction in Trakyo is one payment. Each one is attached to a lead, and through that
lead to the content that earned it — which is what makes your revenue numbers add up to
something you can act on.

## Where transactions come from

Payments arrive in Trakyo automatically once your payment tool is connected. Trakyo
matches each one to the lead who paid, then credits the content in that lead's journey.

Payments taken outside that setup — a bank transfer, cash, an invoice paid by hand — do
not arrive on their own. Someone has to record them, or that revenue is missing from
every report.

## Reading a transaction

Each transaction shows:

- **The amount and currency** as it was actually paid.
- **The lead** who paid it.
- **The content credited** with it, under first touch and last touch.

Amounts in another currency are also converted, so your totals can be read in one
currency.

## When a payment is credited to the wrong content

It happens — a customer bought after a call that came from a video, but their payment
came in through a link that credits something else. You can override the credit on a
single transaction and point it at the right piece of content.

:::warning
An override is a statement about one payment, not a fix for a pattern. If many payments
land on the wrong content, or in **Other**, the cause is in your setup rather than in the
transactions: see [Get attribution you can trust](/docs/help/best-practices/attribution-best-practices).
:::

## Refunds

A refunded payment stays in Trakyo with the refunded amount recorded against it, rather
than disappearing. That keeps your history honest: you can still see the sale was made,
and that it came back.

## Check it worked

Take a payment you know the amount and date of, find it in Trakyo, and confirm the amount
matches your payment tool and the lead is the right person.
